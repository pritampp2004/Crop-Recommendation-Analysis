# Crop-Recommendation-Analysis
This analysis for crop cultivation across 23 different crop varieties. The study leverages a comprehensive dataset encompassing soil nutrient composition (Nitrogen, Phosphorus, Potassium) key environmental parameters (temperature,humidity,pH,rainfall) to establish agronomic baseline conditions for effective crop selection and yield optimization.

Dataset Composition

Scope: 23 crop varieties analyzed across 2,200+ observations
Features Analyzed:

Soil Nutrients: Nitrogen (N), Phosphorus (P), Potassium (K) concentrations
Environmental Variables: Temperature (°C), Humidity (%), Soil pH, Annual Rainfall (mm)
Target Variable: Crop classification (label)
Key Findings
Phosphorus Requirements by Crop Category

The analysis identified distinct phosphorus (P) requirements across crop types, revealing two primary groupings:

High Phosphorus Demand (120+ mg/kg):

Apple and Grapes demonstrate the highest phosphorus requirements (126.66 and 126.46 mg/kg respectively), indicating nutrient-intensive cultivation demands
These fruit crops require intensive soil preparation and supplementation

Moderate Phosphorus Demand (65-85 mg/kg):

Legume crops (lentils, chickpeas, pigeon peas, kidney beans, black gram) cluster around 67-68 mg/kg
These varieties, while requiring moderate phosphorus levels, typically benefit from nitrogen-fixing properties

Low Phosphorus Demand (15-50 mg/kg):

Fruit and vegetable crops (orange, coconut, watermelon, muskmelon, pomegranate) require significantly lower phosphorus (15-18 mg/kg)
Cereal and fiber crops (rice, cotton, jute, maize) fall within 46-48 mg/kg range
Nutrient-Crop Pattern Analysis

The analysis demonstrates that nutrient requirements vary significantly by crop phenotype:

Perennial Fruits: Higher nutrient intensity (apple, grapes, mango)
Legumes: Moderate nutrient requirements with natural nitrogen fixation advantages
Cereals & Pulses: Moderate phosphorus with variable nitrogen needs
Spice & Tropical Crops: Lower overall nutrient concentrations
Analytical Approach

The exploratory analysis employed:

Descriptive statistics and aggregation by crop type
Comparative visualization of nutrient distributions
Mean nutrient requirement calculations stratified by crop variety
Implications & Recommendations

This nutrient-based crop profiling enables:

Precision Agriculture: Targeted fertilizer recommendations based on soil analysis and crop selection
Cost Optimization: Avoid over-fertilization of low-demand crops
Soil Health Management: Align nutrient application to crop-specific requirements
Crop Selection Framework: Match regional soil conditions to suitable crop varieties
Conclusion

The analysis provides agronomic stakeholders with actionable insights for optimizing crop selection decisions and fertilizer management strategies. By understanding nutrient-crop relationships, farmers and agricultural planners can maximize yield potential while maintaining soil health and economic sustainability.
